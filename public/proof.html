Blind test

The weeks above were written by us, so they cannot show our rule is right. A separate analyst agent that never read this code wrote synthetic weeks of its own, with its own answers from first principles: a paid order counts only if not refunded, not a double payment and not in an open dispute; contribution is paid revenue less a 30% cost and PayPal's fee; fewer than 3 orders is too thin to move. Prices from $19 to $199, refunds, disputes, break-even ads. node scripts/eval-blind-scenarios.mjs; the weeks and every answer with its arithmetic are in the repo. The answers are an AI analyst's, not human ground truth: they test the agent and the rule against reasoning we did not write, not against a real shop.

Too few orders

How this is measured

Most small shops live here. The demo shop has 9 paid orders across 4 ads; Trial class · video (3) and Testimonial · carousel (4) have enough to judge, so the rule raises the carousel, keeps the trial class (it covers its costs, but below the average) and keeps the two ads with one paid order each. Keeping is not the whole answer: for each ad the ledger works out, in code (paybackOdds in src/ledger.js), the chance its paid revenue covers its ad spend, a 90% range for its cost per paid order, and, while that is still unclear, how much more spend with no paid order would make "below its ad spend" 90% likely. The model is Gamma-Poisson: paid orders per dollar, a prior worth one order at the shop's own cost per paid order, and the shop's average paid order as the break-even. The budget agent quotes these numbers through the same evidence gate; it cannot make one up. The dashboard shows them in the "Covers its spend?" column.

Eight-week simulation

How this is measured

On the 31 Oct ledger (before the seventh payment) the demo week lost money: $285.00 paid revenue against $326.50 ad spend. What if the shop kept the same weekly budget for 8 weeks and moved it each week? The evidence is thin (6 paid orders across 4 ads), so each of 2,000 runs draws every ad's true paid rate from what the export and PayPal show, and the policies never see that draw; they learn from noisy weeks as a shop would. Three policies: keep the split; follow leads per dollar, which is what ad platforms optimise for; follow PayPal-confirmed paid orders per dollar with the dashboard's own rule (an ad moves only after 3 paid orders, every ad keeps 10%). Figures are paid revenue minus ad spend, before product cost, so not profit. A simulation, not a measurement; it reruns on a real shop's numbers with node scripts/eval-budget-sim.mjs shop.csv.
