How the shop counted, and limits

October 2026: 260M VND (about $10,000) on three ad groups, 12,100 chats, 705 orders paid and kept after refunds. Ranked by cost per chat, the ad report's view: A, B, C. Ranked by money kept per ad dollar: B, C, A.

Customers paid by bank transfer (VietQR); an order counts as paid only after the shop's accountant matched the money received to the order code. Meta records which ad started each chat; staff write that ad group's code into the order book, then match it against payments and refunds. That matching is done by hand today; it is the step Paid Signal does from PayPal's records. The figures are reported by the author from the order book and the ad account; the shop confirms them and published its book (1,214 invoices, 735 payments, 30 refunds, anonymised customer ids, in docs/proof); the ad account's own exports are no longer accessible. "Money" is revenue after refunds, before product, delivery and fees.

Paid Signal's split rule (the same code as the demo: paid orders kept per dollar of spend) would suggest A 53.8% → 17.3%, B 28.8% → 46.3%, C 17.3% → 36.4%. An illustration, not a measured lift; a person decides, and Paid Signal does not change a live Meta budget. The shop's payments did not go through Paid Signal or PayPal: the shop is why it exists, not a result of it. Numbers: merchant-case-2026-10.json.

Method and limits

735 bank transfers and the 30 refunds from its refund book, fed to the same ledger code the demo uses. A: 145 orders, 254M VND · B: 390 orders, 741M VND · C: 170 orders, 322M VND. This checks the arithmetic, the refunds and the order matching, not judgement or ad causality. Without order codes the time rule (same payer, same ad, close together) holds 2 of 7 with the 10-minute checkout window and 7 of 7 with the 60-minute window bank transfers get, a window set after reading this book. Every customer in this book ordered once, so false alarms cannot be measured on it. Run your own book in the browser: /audit.
