Ranked by cost per chat, the ad report's view: A, B, C. Ranked by money paid and kept: B, C, A. The cheapest chats came from the ad that brought the least money.

How the shop counted. Customers paid by bank transfer (VietQR); an order counts as paid only after the shop's accountant matched the money received to the order code. Meta records which ad started each chat; staff write that ad group's code into the order book, then match it against payments and refunds. That matching is done by hand today; it is the step Paid Signal does from PayPal's records. The figures are reported by the author from the order book and the ad account; the account's exports are no longer accessible, so only totals are shown. "Money" is revenue after refunds, before product, delivery and fees.

What Paid Signal would suggest. Its budget rule (the same code as the demo: paid orders kept per dollar of spend, refunds excluded) gives A 54% → 17%, B 29% → 46%, C 17% → 36%; a person decides, and Paid Signal does not change a live Meta budget. The shop's payments did not go through Paid Signal or PayPal: the shop is why it exists, not a result of it. Numbers: merchant-case-2026-10.json.
